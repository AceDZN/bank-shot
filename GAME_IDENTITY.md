# Bank Shot — מכת גדה — game identity

## One line

Trick-shot billiards and curling in a candle-lit Roman bathhouse: the aim line is short, so you read the angles carved into the mosaic, deduce the one you need, set the brass protractor and watch a perfect bank shot drop into the drain in slow motion.

## Core action (without setting or topic)

Turn-based precision shots. Each table shows a few exact angles and a "?" where you need one. You dial an angle to 1°, hold to charge, release. The ball travels straight and bounces off every cushion at the same angle it arrived (the table's stated rule). It drops in, or it misses, and the full path stays on the table as a ghost with the exact angle where your reasoning split from the true line. Three strokes per table, stars for first-stroke shots without a hint.

## Why it isn't another courier, runner or quiz

- No avatar to steer and nothing to carry. The decision is a single number per stroke, and the payoff is watching physics you predicted happen.
- The aim stub is short (≈ a hand's length on the table) and the pocket is far, after one or more bounces, so eyeballing gets close but almost never in. The carved numbers get you in.
- Wrong answers aren't popups. 130° instead of 50° sends the ball the mirror way into the bath with a *plop*; 55° on the crooked corridor misses the drain by a ball's width and the ghost shows you the 4° lean.

## Look

- **Place:** a Roman bath hall at night, inspired by Beit She'an's thermae. Warm candle and oil-lamp light, drifting steam, wet marble that catches highlights, a deep blue-green plunge pool beside each table.
- **Table:** a mosaic floor (deep teal and ochre tesserae) framed by cream marble cushions with rust veins. Gold tile lines in the mosaic are the geometry; the carved angle arcs glow gold with LTR numbers.
- **Ball / stone:** an ivory ball with a thin gold band for the billiard rooms, a granite curling stone with a bronze handle for rooms 6–7.
- **Protractor:** a brass half-disc on the ball, engraved every 10°, a short glowing aim stub, the number in a big LTR readout.
- **Rival:** Decimus (דקימוס), a cheeky bath attendant in a short tunic with a towel over his shoulder. He leans on a column, applauds a clean run, facepalms at a splash.
- **Set pieces:** a bronze lion-head fountain spouting into the pool, column capitals, clay oil lamps on the cushions.
- **Palette:** teal #1f5f63, deep water #0f3b44, warm gold #e2b04a, marble cream #efe3cf, terracotta #b5583a, candle orange #ffb35c. Dark warm shadows, not black.

## Camera

A 3/4 overhead view (about 55° down) that frames the whole table and the pool, slightly orbiting with the protractor. On the strike the camera eases toward the ball's line; the last bounce before the drain plays in slow motion with a push-in. Room 6–7 curling throws use a lower, longer camera along the lane.

## HUD (Hebrew, RTL)

- Top right: room number and name, the fact chip ("זוויות צמודות"), the table rule chip ("חוק השולחן: הכדור יוצא מהגדה באותה זווית שפגע").
- Top left: three stroke tokens (balls), stars, pause, sound, reduced motion.
- Bottom center: the angle readout (`50°`, LTR), − / + buttons (1° and 10°), the hold-to-strike button with a ring power meter.
- Right edge: the hint lamp (lights the relevant pair of angles and names the rule, never the number).
- After a miss: the ghost path, a gold circle on the angle where it split, a one-line explanation, and "שוב" (again).

## Sound

Marble clack on strike, a deep wooden *tock* on each cushion, a hollow *plonk* into the drain with a water gurgle, a big *plop* and splash into the pool, Decimus's whistle, steam hiss ambience, a low lyre loop.

## What it is not

Not a quiz with a ball attached: no typed answers and no "wrong!" cards. Not a toy-industrial diorama, not a courier loop, not neon.
